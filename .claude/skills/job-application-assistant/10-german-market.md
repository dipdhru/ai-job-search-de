# German Market Conventions

Read this whenever the posting is for a role in Germany or is written in German. It adds to
`05-cv-templates.md`, `06-cover-letter-templates.md` and `04-job-evaluation.md` and doesn't
replace them. Where this file and those files differ, this file wins for German applications.

## Which language

- **Posting in German:** write the Anschreiben in German. Write the Lebenslauf in German too, unless the candidate's `CV language` says otherwise *and* the posting states English is the working language.
- **Posting in English** (common at startups, in tech, and at international companies in Berlin and Munich): write everything in English. Don't switch to German to look keen, because a German letter invites a German interview.
- A German text must be fluent. If the candidate's declared German level is below C1, say so before drafting a German letter, and offer an English letter with one German sentence stating their level. Don't produce German that is better than the candidate's own.

## Lebenslauf (CV)

- **Tabellarischer Lebenslauf:** reverse-chronological, two pages maximum. The stock 2-page moderncv template already fits.
- **Section headings** (the `05-cv-templates.md` rule on translating headings applies): `Profil`, `Kernkompetenzen`, `Berufserfahrung`, `Ausbildung`, `Sprachkenntnisse`, `Publikationen`, `Auszeichnungen`, `Referenzen`, and `Auf Anfrage.` under References. Use `Weiterbildung` / `Zertifikate` for certifications and `IT-Kenntnisse` for software skills.
- **Dates:** `MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY` or `seit MM/YYYY` for a current role. Avoid unexplained gaps longer than about three months. Give a one-line reason (Elternzeit, Weiterbildung, Jobsuche nach Standortschließung).
- **Photo:** not required. The AGG (General Equal Treatment Act) discourages employers from asking for one, and many international employers prefer none. The template ships without one. Add one only if the candidate asks.
- **Personal data:** name, city, phone, email and LinkedIn. Date of birth, nationality and marital status are optional and increasingly left out. Include nationality or work-permit status only when it helps (see Work authorization).
- **Language levels:** use CEFR (`Deutsch – B2`, `Englisch – C1 (verhandlungssicher)`). German recruiters read CEFR literally.
- **Ort, Datum and signature** at the end are optional and old-fashioned. Leave them out unless the posting is from the public sector or a traditional Mittelstand company.
- Write numbers the German way in German text, e.g. `1,5 Mio. €` and `30 %`, and escape `%` as `\%` in LaTeX.

## Anschreiben (cover letter)

- **Length:** one page. This is already the template rule.
- **Date line:** `Berlin, 29. Oktober 2026`, with the city first and the month written out.
- **Subject line (Betreff):** bold, with no "Betreff:" label, e.g. `Bewerbung als Data Scientist (m/w/d) – Referenznummer 20390-978522636-S`. Include the reference number whenever the posting gives one. Arbeitsagentur postings always do.
- **Salutation:** `Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt,` or `Sehr geehrter Herr Schmidt,`. If you don't know the gender or name, write `Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,`. Startups often write `Hallo Frau Schmidt,` or use first names. Mirror the posting's tone: if the posting says "du", the letter can too.
- **The first word after the salutation comma is lowercase** unless it is a noun (`Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt,\n\nmit großem Interesse ...`). This is the most common mistake non-native writers make, so check it every time.
- **Opening:** don't start with "hiermit bewerbe ich mich". Every recruiter reads it a hundred times a week. Open with the concrete link between the candidate and the role, following the forward-looking framing rule in `06-cover-letter-templates.md`.
- **Closing:** `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` with **no comma**, then the name. Add `Anlagen: Lebenslauf, Zeugnisse` only when attachments are actually sent.
- **Pronoun of address:** "Sie" is capitalized, and so are `Ihr`, `Ihnen` and `Ihre` when they address the reader.
- **Required facts, when the posting asks for them,** go in the last paragraph before the closing:
  - **Eintrittstermin:** the earliest start date. State the Kündigungsfrist (notice period) if the candidate is employed.
  - **Gehaltsvorstellung:** one gross annual figure in EUR (`Bruttojahresgehalt`), or a narrow range, e.g. `Meine Gehaltsvorstellung liegt bei 72.000 € brutto jährlich.` If the posting asks and the letter skips it, many recruiters screen the application out. Base the figure on the benchmark step in `04-job-evaluation.md`. For public employers, cite the pay grade (`TV-L E13`, `TVöD E12`) instead of a figure.

## Attachments (Anlagen)

- German employers expect **Arbeitszeugnisse** (references from past employers) and degree certificates. For a portal application, offer to combine CV, letter and certificates into one PDF under 5 MB, ordered as Anschreiben, Lebenslauf, then Zeugnisse, newest first.
- Arbeitszeugnisse are written in a coded language. `stets zu unserer vollsten Zufriedenheit` means top grade, and `zu unserer Zufriedenheit` means below average. If the candidate provides theirs, read them for what they actually say before quoting any of them.

## Evaluating fit (additions to 04-job-evaluation.md)

- `(m/w/d)` or `(w/m/d)` in a title is a legal gender-neutrality marker. It carries no information about the role.
- **German as a requirement:** `verhandlungssicher` or `fließend` means C1 or higher. `gute Deutschkenntnisse` usually means B2. `Deutschkenntnisse von Vorteil` is a nice-to-have, not a gate. Apply the Language Gate with these readings.
- **Contract:** `befristet` means fixed-term and `unbefristet` means permanent. A `Probezeit` (probation, usually six months) is standard and not a red flag.
- **Working-time terms:** Vollzeit means full time, Teilzeit part time, Werkstudent a working student (limited to about 20 hours a week during the semester, and enrolled students only), and Minijob means marginal employment. Exclude Werkstudent and Praktikum roles unless the candidate is a student or explicitly wants them.
- **Zeitarbeit / Arbeitnehmerüberlassung** (temp agency) and **Personalvermittlung** (recruiting agency) postings hide the real employer. Flag them, because the client company is often findable and worth applying to directly.

## Work authorization

When the candidate is not an EU/EEA/Swiss citizen, run the eligibility check in `04-job-evaluation.md` with these routes in mind:
- **EU Blue Card:** this needs a recognized degree and a salary above the yearly threshold. The threshold is lower for shortage occupations, which include IT and engineering. It changes every January, so look up the current figure on make-it-in-germany.com before stating it. Never quote it from memory.
- **Chancenkarte** (Opportunity Card, a points-based job-seeker visa) and **§18b** (skilled worker with an academic degree).
- **Degree recognition:** check the anabin database or the ZAB (Central Office for Foreign Education). A degree listed as `H+` is generally recognized. If the candidate's degree status is unknown, list it as an open question. Don't guess.
- **IT specialists** without a degree can qualify through professional experience. Look up the current rules before relying on this.
- If eligibility is unclear, say so and point to make-it-in-germany.com or the employer's HR department. This is not legal advice.

## Salary context

- Salaries are quoted as gross per year (`brutto jährlich`) in EUR. Net pay is roughly 55-65% of gross, depending on tax class. Don't convert unless asked.
- Many public and some private employers pay by collective agreement (`Tarifvertrag`: TV-L, TVöD, IG Metall). Their pay tables are public. Look up the posted grade rather than estimating.
- `salary_lookup.py` needs a user-supplied dataset (see `tools/README_SALARY_TOOL.md`). Public German sources that could feed it are the Entgeltatlas of the Bundesagentur für Arbeit (median pay by occupation and region) and, for tech roles, the published salary reports that recruiters release each year.
